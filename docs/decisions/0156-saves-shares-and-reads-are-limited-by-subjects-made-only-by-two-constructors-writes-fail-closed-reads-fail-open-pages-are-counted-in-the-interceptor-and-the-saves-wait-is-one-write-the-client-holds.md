## Decisions

### 1. A bucket's subject is made only by two constructors, and each remembers what it made

Four kinds of subject exist: an IPv4 block, an IPv6 block, the shared bucket and **a write credential's hash**; the constructors are the only way to make one, and **each remembers what it made, by identity**. The evaluator answers `judgement-failed`, with no store call, for a subject it did not make or a policy that is not one of the closed set's own (G-S1.1); a hash that is not a string is refused before its pattern is asked (G-S1.2); a purpose that is not the list's own member is *misconfigured* (G-S1.3). *Why:* a type mark binds the compiler only, and a cast could be written in the save's route. **No text a caller chooses becomes a subject, except through a credential's hash or an address's parsed bits** (criterion 9). *Must not:* a token, a visitor's value, a share's token or a design's id as a subject. The mint's key is unchanged and held by its pinned answer.

### 2. The save's second bucket is the write credential, by its hash, never a design's id (G-S0.1)

The security reader said no to the plan's *"the second bucket is the design"*: the two checks before the limiter prove nothing (any text in a cookie named for a design passes both [src `write-token.ts`]), so whoever knows a design's address, which is not a secret (ADR 0114), could spend its second figure from their own network and stop its owner's saves. **The subject is what `readWriteTokenHash` already hands the route, under a kind of its own.** Only the holder can address that bucket; every real save of a design presents the same one; a mint does not change it (D11). In ADR 0019's terms, d5's *"Session"* row becomes **one browser's saves of one design**, since M8 has no session cookie (a dated note is on d3). The hash appears only inside the keyed digest, in no log.

### 3. Windows are data; the evaluator judges one bucket and a save is a sequence

A window is data: the Chișinău day as 8F has it, or **a whole number of minutes on the UTC clock, sixty being the hour**, from one whole boundary to the next, with the seconds to its end. The store keeps one row for a bucket and a window, so a sliding hour has nothing to live in; the cost is D10's (twice a figure across one boundary). **The evaluator stays as 8F left it, one bucket.** A save is a **sequence of judgements**, the first refusal or failure ending it and no later step hit. The plan's earlier list-in-one-statement is withdrawn: nothing in this wave lists two buckets. Several buckets in one statement, the stricter deciding, are for a wave that needs them.

### 4. Reads fail open, writes fail closed

ADR 0019 d5, kept. A **write** (mint, share write, save) that cannot be judged answers `503` and writes nothing. A **read** that cannot be judged is **served**: the deadline is its caller's, **three seconds for a write, 300 ms for a read** (the architect's figure), after which the read is served; one log line for a process and a cause, not one for a request. **Under no network source a read is not counted at all: no store call, no row**, because the world sharing 600 reads protects nothing and could stop every page (G-S0.3). A refusal of a read is the same for an owner and a stranger (ADR 0114 H5). A read's judge is the share's and the API's only; it asks before any lookup. Share writes and saves under *none* are named interims, as the mint's is (decision 11).

### 5. Pages are counted and refused in the request interceptor, and only there

A page's status cannot change once it streams [doc, the S3b report, `streaming.md:613`], and saying *too many requests* under a `200` was not accepted, so the framework leaves one place: the interceptor `src/proxy.ts`. **It counts, and waits for the judgement, for a counted request under a named source and nowhere else.** What counts: `/` and a locale's root, a design's page and its three steps, a share's view; a document and a navigation's data fetch alike (the interceptor cannot tell them apart [doc, `proxy.md:474`] and each runs the lookup); only a request it would pass on. Not counted: the locale's redirect, the case-variant `404`, a static file, anything under `/api` (its route counts). **The limiter is reached by a dynamic import inside the interceptor's own `try`, asked only when a source is named**; under *none* the interceptor loads nothing new and answers at once, as before, and no message file enters its own graph. Anything thrown, unjudged or late is served, with one line for a process and a cause; no input makes the interceptor answer `500` (criterion 10). **A refusal is `429`, `Retry-After` from 1 to 3,600, `no-store`, the headers a page's answer already gets, and a small HTML body with no script and no style**, in the path's language, with one sentence from one new leaf (`designLoadError.tooManyRequests`, a draft under Q10 (a)) and **a plain link that loads the same address again**, worded by a leaf of the page's own (GC-R2.4; a surface's words are its own). *Its cost, said plainly:* where a source is named each counted request waits one round trip of about 50 ms [relayed], 300 ms at most; a counted page's median on the built server was about 52 to 57 ms against about 5 ms with no source [developer-run]. *Not accepted:* a count taken after the design is loaded, a check in the browser, a redirect naming a design, a `200` that says too many requests.

### 6. The escaped-locale rule: a locale is written one way (G-S2.1), and the matcher's two families (G-S3.1)

A locale written with a percent-escape was served its page and not counted, and a share view so reached went out **without its three headers** [relayed, QA-run on a built server; 11 tests red]: the interceptor knows a locale only as written, the framework matches it decoded. **The rule: a first segment that is not itself a locale but is one once decoded and lowered is the `404` a case variant already gets, refused before any header is chosen and before anything is counted; a segment that cannot be decoded is left to the framework.** *Not taken: decoding, then counting and serving*, because two spellings of one page would then live in every rule that follows. Only a route's *parameters* are decoded; fixed segments match as written; dot segments are resolved before the interceptor reads the path [QA-run]; 162 spellings of each locale, none both valid to the framework and let through [security, ran, matcher probe]. **G-S3.1:** a path ending in a static file's extension never reached the interceptor and the framework still routed `/ro/d/abc123.png` to the design's page, whose lookup then ran uncounted; **the matcher hands the interceptor the design and share families whatever they end in** [no stored design or share view is reachable so, since the id alphabet has no dot and a token is twelve letters and digits [src]; a flood had only to end its address in `.png`].

### 7. The prune

`GET /api/cron/prune-rate-limits`, behind `CRON_SECRET` through `isAuthorizedCronRequest` (`401` without it); `30 3 * * *` **added to `apps/web/vercel.json` after the backup's, an additional scheduled job from the next deploy**, add-only and told to the owner. It removes only windows that ended **an hour or more** before the server's own clock (never a request's value), **stepped forward, oldest first**: once at twenty-five hours before the clock, then an hour at a time up to one hour before it, so a run cut short has still removed the oldest (G-S0.2). Before a day's rows go it writes **the day's totals: one line holding the Chișinău day's date and three counts** (how many buckets, how many above the mint's figure, the largest count), no key and no block, from one new read in the store's port that changes no existing statement; **a day with no bucket left writes no line** (a zero is not a count; GL-R1.1); **a failed totals read is one line and the prune still runs, and the night answers `503`** so it is seen (G-S2.2, GL-R1.3); naming the days is inside the guarded part, so a runtime that cannot name the zone is `503` with its code and line (GL-R1.2). A prune that fails is a different route from the backup's, so neither can report the other's failure. The number it logs is a lower bound. *Must not:* a table but the counters, a window not yet over, a design, ever. **The delete statement is the store's, unchanged** (ADR 0149's thirty runs rest on it). *Recorded, with its trigger:* a day whose totals read failed has no totals, ever (GL-R2.1; trigger: a failed-prune line whose step is 1 or 2); a flood's rows are one statement, not batched (trigger: a prune that fails for time).

### 8. The save: two steps, in an order of writing

A `PUT` or `PATCH /api/designs/{id}` asks **after** the two refusals that are not counted (no write-token cookie for that design, or no revision) and **before the body is read**: the block's bucket first; **only a request the block passed is counted against its credential**. The **instant and the window are taken once for both steps**, so a save judged across an hour's turn states one wait; the wait is checked against the policy's own window; anything that is not *allow* is a refusal or `503`, `judgement-failed` included. `429` with `SAVE_LIMIT_REACHED`, `503` with `SAVE_UNAVAILABLE`, no count, block or remainder. **At most one refusal line for a process and a policy** (G-S2.3). *Must not, on a refusal:* the body is read, a row is written, a cookie is set, cleared or refreshed; nothing of the limiter runs between the write and the answer. **One statement for both buckets was not taken, and the two steps are an order of *writing*, not only of judging:** the store's hit does take several keys, but a request its block refused would then still write its credential's row, and a token is the caller's to invent, so rows would be unbounded. *Cost, accepted:* a save took **61.2 ms without the limiter and 158.2 ms with it**, two store round trips of about 48 ms one after the other [developer-run, production build, 30 saves at one a second]; the stop was written against QA's 166 ms and not reached; against its own *before* it is 2.6 times. **It stands because the customer does not wait on it:** the client sends one save a second at most. *Trigger:* a save's time on a deployment that a customer waits on, the review's wait first; or Q37 answered (d), which removes the second step. *The closure then:* one statement that hits the credential only when the block's count is at or under its figure, with its own lock-order proof. A read of the current revision after a conflict is a design's load, which can itself be refused; it is held to the failure the editor already shows.

### 9. No e2e twin for the credential's bucket; the twins' figures

Production's figure applies to the credential in the suite: a design is saved nine times at most [relayed, QA's measurement], and a test that reached it would be refused loudly. A test's cleanup holds the count of each credential row it removes and fails when one is above half the figure. **Twins' figures** (the 8F rule, *twice, to the next hundred, fail above half*, left a margin of four): **a twin's figure is three times what one whole run makes, rounded up to the next hundred, and the teardown still fails above half.** Share writes **500**, saves by block **1,200**, **the mint 1,600** (changing 8F's 1,100). Measured at the last whole run: mints 528 of 1,600 (five under a third, 533), saves 337 of 1,200, share writes 71 of 500 [QA-run, at `a72c3a82`]. **The wave that passes a third raises the constant.** One flag chooses every twin; the e2e notice is written when any twin first decides, in a sentence true of each: `rate limit: an e2e policy is in use (LEMNARIA_E2E_RATE_LIMIT_POLICY); its figure is not the production one`. A test-only read in the persistence package answers the write-token hashes of the designs whose ids it is handed, guarded as the two test-only deletes are; each test's cleanup uses it before its designs go. **One module now holds the guard "this process is a test runner"** (`require-test-runner.ts`; GC-R3.2): it had been written three times and meant two things; **all three accept Playwright's own runner**, and what keeps them from the application is the connection, which is the guarded test database's alone. *The ports of the second servers are named in one place:* 4322 the production policy's figure, 4323 QA's refused mint, 4324 a named source, and a fourth, 4325, for QA's own spec (the plan's first-round fix round named a fourth port; its exact use is as the coordinator's hand-over gave it, not re-read in code by the scribe).

### 10. The client holds one waiting write

A save's `429` carrying `SAVE_LIMIT_REACHED` is a failure of its own, holding the wait; any other `429` is what it was (a firewall's, M9's). The coalescer holds **one waiting write**: it waits the stated time **from the answer's arrival, never less**, between one second and the window's length, **and a fixed minute when the header is absent, unreadable or above the window** (the clamp, G-S0.5: tested over absent, empty, negative, fractional, exponent, date, padded, above 3,600, 23 digits and non-ASCII digits; `0` waits 1 [security, ran]). Edits made meanwhile join it; a save refused twice is still one; there is no second timer and no request before the wait ends. The store carries `error` and a time, and **one predicate, `isSavePaused`, is asked by the panel, the review and the store** (the store keeps no time beside any status but `error`; GC-R3.6; GC-R4.1 below). The editor's one sentence says saving is paused until a stated time and the changes are kept on this screen: **a draft under Q10 (a), in `ro` and `ru`; the Russian draft speaks informally here and formally on the refused page** (to be settled when ADR 0095's re-sign is made). **A design loaded while its own write waits is given the waiting design and its wait, not the server's copy and *saved*** (GL-R3.1, ruled before it was shown reachable; **reached and fixed** [QA-run: its traces 7 of 7, every attack green]): the screen shows it unsaved with the sentence, the waiting write stays the one write on the one timer, and a later edit joins it. *Not taken:* telling the wait to the new screen and still dropping the edit, which is honest and loses work. The server takes the save's code and window from the client's module, as the mint's does, and a test ties the client's window to the policy's (GC-R3.1, G-S4.1).

### 11. Named interims under *none*

Under no network source: a **read** is never counted (decision 4); a **save** is a named interim (the world shares the network's figure, and a script of that many requests stops every save until the window turns); a **share write** is a third (GL-R1.5). Acceptable only while nothing takes customers; M9's launch sentence already requires a named platform. **Preview and the owner's hand checks meet it first unless he sets `CLIENT_NETWORK_SOURCE` there.**

### 12. The closing rule (the architect's, open to the owner)

After round 2 of the first pass, a finding was fixed inside 8G only if a counted request is served uncounted, or a refused one served; a share's view goes out without one of its three headers; the prune removes a window that has not been over for an hour, touches another table, or fails unseen; or an address, a block, a key, the secret or a count leaves. For the second pass, two clauses were added: a save is refused that should be stored, or stored that should be refused; **or an edit made in the browser is neither sent nor shown as unsaved, without a full load.** Anything else is recorded with its trigger. C2-1 was outside it (below).

## Owner's questions (all recorded; none blocks building)

- **Q36.** How often may one network create or revoke a share link? No record gives a figure. *Recommended:* 60 an hour per block for the two together; asking a link's status counts as a read. Built as a named provisional constant.
- **Q37, the save limits against the measured pace** [the plan's wording]: *saving is limited to 600 an hour for a network and 300 an hour for one browser's design (ADR 0019; the owner's answer of 2026-10-07). Measured: an ordinary brisk session saves about 7 times a minute and reaches 300 in about 42 minutes; someone dragging and tapping without pause saves 54 times a minute and reaches it in under 6 [QA-run, relayed: the editor sends at most 53 to 54 a minute]. Saving then stops until the hour turns, up to 54 minutes, with the work on the screen and not stored.* (a) keep both; (b) 3,600 an hour for a browser's design and 10,800 for a network; (c) the same rate in ten-minute windows, 600 and 1,800, so no honest editor is stopped and a stop lasts ten minutes at most; (d) limit saves by network alone. **The architect recommends (c).** Built as named provisional constants at ADR 0019's values, so his answer changes four constants and no code. Added by the second pass: a pause can last up to the window; a reload or a closed tab loses the waiting write; and the second round trip goes if (d).

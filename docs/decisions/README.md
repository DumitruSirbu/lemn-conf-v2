# Architecture Decision Records

This directory is the project's decision log. In this repository an ADR records **our decisions and
findings** — why the code is shaped the way it is, what was tried and rejected, and what a review
turned up that changed a plan. It is not where the product's behavioural rules live: those are
`docs/design/DEVELOPMENT-SPEC.md`, cited by section and row (e.g. "§5.2", "§11 row 19"), never
duplicated into a decision record. A dated ADR supersedes earlier prose anywhere else in the
repo — `docs/CONFIGURATION_MODEL.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE_PROPOSAL.md`, milestone plans — but it
does not replace the behavioural spec as the source of truth for what the product does.

Read `docs/STATUS.md` first for where the project currently stands. Come here for the "why" behind a
piece of code; come here before re-deciding anything, since a dated record must not be silently
overturned.

| [0031](0031-a-resize-moves-a-whole-column-into-adjacent-empty-space.md) | A resize moves a whole column, and is funded by adjacent empty space | Accepted | Decision | M5 wave 5C — `setWidth` and the column update | **Supersedes §5.4's donor rule.** Implementing §5.4 as written produced **two reachable resizes in the whole shipped kitchen** — both sides of one boundary — because it takes the entire delta from one neighbour whose residue must be a legal width, and ADRs 0029/0030's 200 mm-granular sets almost never allow that. Every defect patched that day (the 1000 mm two-door, the 400 mm cabinet labelled as two doors, illegal millimetre-set residues) is downstream of a resize **shrinking a cabinet nobody selected**. Now: selecting any cabinet resizes its **whole column** at every level, funded by an **adjacent empty column** which narrows or widens to match; a column's legal widths are the intersection of its members' sets; no adjacent empty column means refused. | [0033](0033-door-count-is-derived-from-width-not-declared.md) | Door count is derived from width, not declared as a separate entry | Accepted | Decision | M5 wave 5C3 — merging the door-count entries | ADRs 0029, 0030 and 0031 each fixed one case and opened the next: a base that can be 400 under a `wall-2door` that cannot means the column can never be 400. **The geometry was never the problem** — `w > 0.45 ? 2 : 1` has always derived the door count correctly; the **label** is what was declared, and what therefore lied. `wall-2door` at 400 mm is a correct one-door cabinet wearing a two-door name. So the door-count pairs merge into one entry each (`[200, 300, 400, 600, 800]`), and the door count **and its label** are derived at the same constant the builder branches on — one threshold, so they cannot disagree. Both product rules survive exactly, restated as one fact: below the threshold one door, above it two. No migration and no surprise, because there is no other entry to migrate to. What still constrains a column is only **appliances** (hob 600, dishwasher 450/600, sink 600/800, fridge 700) — a fact about hobs, not a modelling failure, and ADR 0032 gives it somewhere to be said. Supersedes the *mechanism* of 0029 and 0030, not their rules. **Amended 2026-08-27**: a hinged cabinet's widths are **two ranges** — one door 200–400, two doors 600–800 — not five discrete values, because the discrete set removed the hinged base cabinet from the 700 mm opening under the fridge where the builder makes an honest two-door. Chips still render the five modular widths. **Cabinetry is continuous within its door-count range; appliances are discrete.** **Amended again the same day**: logic review swept what the waves removed and found the pattern a fourth time — `base-sink` lost the 700 mm opening with no record naming it, and the `hood` refused where every other discrete row narrows. A sink base is **cabinetry with a fixed aperture** (range 560–800; ADR 0017's floor untouched), and the hood now installs a 600 in a wider slot, centred. The generalisation: **a list of sizes that is the customer's menu is not automatically the engine's bound** — chips are what may be picked, ranges what may be built, a floor what may not be crossed, and `CatalogOffer` now publishes all three separately. |
| [0034](0034-width-chips-are-column-scoped-and-judged-by-the-command.md) | §9's width chips are column-scoped, and each one is judged by running the command | Accepted | Decision | M5 wave 5D — wiring the width section | M4 shipped every non-current chip `blocked`, truthfully, because no resize command existed. 5C built one; this record is what replaces that pin. Two things change. **The chip row is the column's, not the body's** — `describeSelection` answers per placement while ADR 0031 resizes a whole column, so the panel was rendering 76 chips where the engine recognises 62, over-offering and under-offering by the same cause: the dishwasher's 450 that the `wall-cabinet` above it cannot match (450 falls in ADR 0033's gap between one door and two; the open shelf between them is a ladder and accepts it), and the 300 a standard-ladder base does not veto. Two consequences worth knowing: `active` is read off the body rather than out of `WIDTH_UNCHANGED`, because a column with an immovable member refuses every width including its own and would otherwise render no active chip at all; and the **only** thing that suppresses the section is the selected body resolving no set of its own — a column that cannot agree on a width still renders chips carrying the engine's reason, whichever way it fails to agree, which is ADR 0032 applied to the width section rather than contradicted by it. Nothing became resizable — the shipped kitchen has no empty column at load, so every chip in it is still blocked, now for a reason about space rather than about a missing command. |
| [0035](0035-width-is-a-bounded-stepper-not-a-menu-of-sizes.md) | Width is a bounded ±50 mm stepper, not a menu of sizes | Accepted | Decision | M5 — supersedes wave 5D's chip row, gates 5E | Six ADRs in a row (0026, 0029, 0030, 0031, 0033, 0034) each answered *which numbers belong in this entry's size list*, and together described a model fighting itself: the engine had already been pushed to ranges while the catalogue hand-maintained a menu and the panel rendered it as chips. Width becomes **`+50` / `−50` relative to the cabinet's current width**, clamped to a per-entry floor and ceiling, with the button at a bound disabled and carrying its reason. Stepping is **relative, not snapped to a grid** — the shipped kitchen holds a 570 corner return and a 750 fridge column, and snapping would rewrite bodies nobody touched. Entries whose width is genuinely fixed (dishwasher 450/600, fridge, oven) keep a **discrete choice and show no stepper**, so §9 has two width controls; the appliance is the fixed thing and the cabinet is built around it. Forced alongside: **two doors start at 500 mm** (`TWO_DOOR_MIN_WIDTH_MM` 450 → 500, `>` → `>=`), because a stepper reaches 500 and 550 where `w > 450` would make two 250 mm doors, and relative stepping reaches 470 as well. The hood's range is 600–800. **A column is one width at every level** and a resize squares up a mixed column rather than refusing it; **shrinking creates adjacent empty space**, which is what makes the stepper usable given ADR 0031's consequence that nothing is resizable until something is deleted. **Price becomes a formula over width, never a table keyed on sizes**, so the step can move from 50 to 25 without rework. ADR 0034's engine seam survives intact; its chip row and pinned counts do not. |

## Supersessions

Beyond the index's "Status" column, these are the specific claims that were overtaken by a later
record. The superseded text stays in place, struck through, with a pointer to the record that
replaced it — nothing is deleted or rewritten silently.

- **ADR 0001** decision 3 (windows/doors in the first release) → superseded by **ADR 0003** (not
  present in the design reference; retired from near-term scope).
- **ADR 0001** decision 7, the "temporary persistence adapter" half → superseded by **ADR 0002**
  (a server-side Postgres database, not a temporary adapter).
